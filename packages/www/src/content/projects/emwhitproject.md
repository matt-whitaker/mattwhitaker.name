The Emwhit Project is the home for my music — a solo, self-taught songwriting and production
outlet I run alongside my engineering career. Where the day job is logical and problem-oriented,
this is the creative counterpart: a place to share my voice without folding it into a software
portfolio.

The site itself is deliberately small, built with Astro and Tailwind — the same stack behind this
personal site, and for the same reasons. Astro's static output means no client-side framework
overhead for what's fundamentally a handful of pages, and Tailwind's utility classes make it fast
to style and restyle without maintaining a separate design system. For a single-purpose music
site, that combination adds up to quick load times and very little ongoing maintenance once it
ships — important for something that isn't the main project I'm actively iterating on.

"Minimalist" isn't a hedge, it's the actual brief. The site doesn't need to make a broad case for
anything; it needs to get a visitor to the music with as little friction as possible. Every
decision in the build — the framework, the styling approach, the scope of the pages themselves —
follows from that constraint rather than working against it.

mattwhitaker.name links out to it directly: the homepage has its own Music section introducing
the project before sending visitors on to the full site. Keeping the two separate, rather than
folding the music into a subsection of the portfolio, lets each stay its own thing — one site
for the engineering work, one for the music, linked to each other but not competing for the same
tone or audience.
